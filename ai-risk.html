AI Career Risk Report

Report date: . AI changes fast. If you are reading this months later, treat these numbers as a snapshot and ask NextPath for the latest update.
How to read the numbers. Each percentage is our estimate of the share of entry-level openings in that field that AI is likely to absorb by about 2035. The orange bar is the risk if you ignore AI. The green bar is the risk if you learn AI inside your own field. Bars show a range because nobody can predict this to one number.
Be honest with students: the ranges are built from research direction plus our judgment. No study measured these exact figures. The "with AI" range is our estimate of how much learning AI helps, based on the pattern in the evidence below. Treat them as guidance, not a guarantee.
What the evidence says. PwC 2026 (1 billion job ads, 27 countries): workers with AI skills earn a 62% wage premium, AI-skill jobs grow 69% vs 9% for all jobs, and the most AI-exposed companies grew headcount 52% vs 36%. India: entry-level IT roles are reported down up to 25% while NASSCOM sees demand for about 1 million AI-skilled people by 2027 against 5 to 6.5 lakh available. Quess Corp found 6.63 lakh of India's 9.2 lakh AI professionals simply added AI to an existing job. IMF: 26% of India's workforce is exposed to generative AI, 12% at direct risk. Pattern: routine entry-level work is being cut, while people who use AI to amplify their own field are paid more.
Kerala and India note (our reasoning, not measured data). IT hubs like Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Kochi Infopark feel change fastest. Government jobs, licensed professions, Gulf nursing recruitment and physical trades change slower. A student in a small Kerala town faces the same AI tools but usually a slower local adoption curve. Your skill level matters more than your district.
Why each number? We look at the entry-level work in a field and ask how much of it is digital, repetitive and rule-based (AI is strong there) versus physical, licensed or trust-based (AI is weak there). Each course lists both sides under "AI takes over" and "Stays human". The task lists are our reasoning from the research, so a range such as 45-60% means roughly that share of beginner-level work could be absorbed, not that the same share of people lose jobs.
Sources used: PwC 2026 Global AI Jobs Barometer; NASSCOM figures via Storyboard18 and Masai School; Quess Corp AI Workforce Analysis 2026; IMF estimate via Observer Research commentary; Microsoft Research exposure list (via ApiDots); a peer-reviewed Springer study on nursing automation; Brookings/OECD summaries via MajorMatch; Hareesh V Das guide "20 Careers Harder to Fully Automate" (PDF tag). Some legal and media figures come from aggregator sites, so treat them as upper bounds. Engineering branch trends were checked against Careers360 and AICTE-linked reports. Course lists were checked against the Amrita Allied Health Sciences prospectus 2026-27, Sri Ramachandra Faculty of Allied Health Sciences, DY Patil School of Allied Health Sciences and Careers360. College names are suggestions only. Verify seats, approval and fees directly with each college. More courses are added with each update.
